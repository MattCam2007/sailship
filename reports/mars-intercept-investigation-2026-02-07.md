# Mars Intercept Accuracy Investigation

**Date:** 2026-02-07
**Phase:** Discovery (Phase 1)

## 1. Executive Summary

The encounter markers ("ghost planets") show where Mars will be when your predicted trajectory crosses Mars's orbital radius. However, the player arrived at the crossing point at a different time than predicted, resulting in Mars being ahead of the ship. The root cause is that **the encounter marker system and the actual ship physics use fundamentally different trajectory calculations**, and the encounter marker only checks one specific orbital radius rather than tracking the continuously-evolving closest approach.

## 2. The Problem in Plain English

Think of it like this: Mars orbits the Sun in an ellipse, ranging from 1.38 AU (closest to Sun) to 1.67 AU (farthest from Sun). The encounter marker system works by asking:

> "At what point does my predicted spiral path cross 1.524 AU (Mars's average distance from the Sun)?"

Once it finds that crossing point, it looks up where Mars will be at that exact moment and shows a ghost planet there.

**The problem has three parts:**

### Problem 1: Only checking the average distance, not the actual distance

Mars's orbit is eccentric (e = 0.0934). Its actual distance from the Sun varies by ~0.28 AU between perihelion and aphelion. The encounter marker only checks the semi-major axis (1.524 AU) — not Mars's actual current distance. So if Mars is currently at 1.45 AU or 1.62 AU, the crossing detection fires at the wrong radius entirely.

A previous fix attempted to check perihelion/aphelion radii too, but this was reverted because it caused ghost planet "snapping" (the ghost jumping between positions when small sail adjustments flipped which radius crossing "won" the deduplication). So currently, only the semi-major axis is checked (`intersectionDetector.js:500`).

### Problem 2: The predicted trajectory diverges from actual ship physics

The encounter marker uses a trajectory generated by `trajectory-predictor.js`, which:
- Simulates the ship forward in fixed time steps (every 2 hours at 12 steps/day)
- Applies sail thrust at each step to modify orbital elements
- Generates ~200-1500 position points over the prediction window

But the **actual game physics** (`shipPhysics.js`) recalculates thrust every single frame at the game's time warp rate. Over weeks or months, small thrust accumulation differences compound. The trajectory predictor is an approximation — it was designed for visualization ("where am I roughly heading"), not for precise arrival time prediction.

For a Mars transfer taking 200+ days, even a 0.1% error in accumulated thrust per step compounds into days of timing error at the crossing point.

### Problem 3: The ghost shows where Mars will be at crossing time, but doesn't account for approach geometry

The encounter marker answers: "When my path crosses Mars's orbital radius, where is Mars?"

But what actually matters for an intercept is: "When will I get closest to Mars, considering that both I and Mars are moving continuously?" These are different questions. Your ship doesn't teleport to the crossing point — it spirals gradually outward (or inward). During that spiral, Mars is also moving along its orbit at ~24 km/s. The encounter marker gives you a snapshot in time, but the actual closest approach may happen before or after the radius crossing.

The course solver (`evaluate-trajectory.js`) does this correctly — it tracks the actual ship-to-planet distance at every time step and finds the true minimum. But the encounter marker display uses the simpler radius-crossing method.

## 3. Technical Details

### Systems Involved

| System | File | What It Does |
|--------|------|-------------|
| Trajectory Predictor | `trajectory-predictor.js` | Generates predicted ship path (200-1500 points) |
| Intersection Detector | `intersectionDetector.js` | Finds where trajectory crosses orbital radii |
| Encounter Renderer | `renderer.js:1108-1238` | Draws ghost planets and time labels |
| Course Solver | `evaluate-trajectory.js` | Finds actual intercepts (used for AUTO tab, not markers) |
| Ship Physics | `shipPhysics.js` | Real-time physics updates (frame-by-frame) |

### Key Numbers for Mars

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Semi-major axis (a) | 1.524 AU |
| Eccentricity (e) | 0.0934 |
| Perihelion | 1.382 AU |
| Aphelion | 1.666 AU |
| Orbital range | 0.284 AU variation |
| SOI radius | 0.00386 AU (~577,000 km) |
| Mars orbital speed | ~24 km/s |

### The Radius-Only Check

`intersectionDetector.js:494-505`:
```
// Check only the semi-major axis radius.
// Multi-radius checking (perihelion/aphelion) was removed because it causes
// ghost planet position snapping...
const crossing = findRadiusCrossing(p1, p2, r1, r2, a);
```

Only the semi-major axis (1.524 AU) is checked, even though Mars could be anywhere from 1.382 to 1.666 AU.

### Trajectory Resolution

The trajectory predictor runs at 12 steps/day (2-hour segments). Bisection refinement narrows crossing times to ~2 seconds precision within a segment. But the underlying trajectory is still an approximation — thrust is applied discretely every 2 hours rather than continuously.

### What the Course Solver Does Differently

`evaluate-trajectory.js:246-256` checks **three radii** for eccentric orbits:
```
if (targetE > 0.05 && targetE < 0.95) {
    targetRadii = [perihelion, targetA, aphelion];
}
```

And critically, it also tracks the **actual minimum distance** between ship and planet at every step (`lines 295-303`), regardless of radius crossings.

## 4. Gap Analysis

### Missing Capabilities
- [ ] Encounter markers don't use the closest-approach data that already exists (`detectClosestApproaches()`)
- [ ] Encounter markers only check semi-major axis, not Mars's actual position
- [ ] No visual indication of intercept confidence/accuracy on the marker itself
- [ ] Trajectory predictor divergence from actual physics is not communicated to player

### The Core Design Tension

The encounter marker system was designed for **visual trajectory planning** (adjust sail, watch ghost move) rather than **precise intercept prediction**. It answers "roughly when do I cross this orbit?" which is useful early in planning. But as you approach the target and need precision, it becomes misleading because:

1. The displayed time is based on a trajectory approximation
2. The radius check doesn't match Mars's actual position in its eccentric orbit
3. The ghost shows a snapshot, not the evolving approach geometry

## 5. Open Questions

- [ ] Should encounter markers use `detectClosestApproaches()` data instead of (or in addition to) radius crossings?
- [ ] Should the ghost planet show Mars's position at the predicted closest approach time rather than the radius crossing time?
- [ ] Should there be a confidence indicator that degrades as the crossing gets further into the future?
- [ ] Would re-enabling multi-radius checking (with better deduplication) improve accuracy for eccentric orbits?
- [ ] Should the trajectory predictor use higher resolution (more steps) for the portion of trajectory near planetary encounters?
